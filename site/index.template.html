GNOME Shell extension

Root-level good luck for your desktop.

It hangs there over your desktop and sways. Grab it and it swings — the cord stretches while you pull and whips when you let go, because it is an actual pendulum rather than a looping animation. Call it down and it performs a small ritual belonging to that particular charm.

It does nothing useful. That is the entire point.

The one on this page is the real thing. Pull it.

The daruma keeps score

Call it down once to make a wish and it paints its left eye. Call it again when the wish lands and it paints the right.

That state survives reboots, which means the doll on your screen is quietly keeping a record of what you said you would do.

Install

Download the zip from the releases page, then:

# from the downloaded zip
gnome-extensions install --force sudocharm@rahul.local.shell-extension.zip
gnome-extensions enable sudocharm@rahul.local

Then log out and back in. GNOME Shell cannot load a new extension into a running Wayland session. There is no way around it and it catches everyone once.

Bless a commit

It sits on the session bus, so anything that can run a command can reach it.

# .git/hooks/post-commit
sudocharm bless "$(git rev-parse --short HEAD)"

# or make it earn its keep
pytest && sudocharm bless || sudocharm flick 9

The wrapper is a few lines over gdbus, so nothing needs installing on the far end.

How it hangs

Three coupled systems, integrated every frame: the swing, the cord's elasticity, and the charm's own lag behind the cord.

θ''       = −(g/L)·sin θ − damping·θ' + breeze(t)
stretch'' = −k·stretch − c·stretch' + ω²·L·0.06
lag''     = −k₂·(lag − θ) − c₂·lag'

L is the live cord length, so hauling the charm down genuinely slows its period. Gravity is tuned by eye rather than taken from physics — real gravity at screen scale swings far too slowly to read as a small object on a short string. The breeze is three sine waves with periods of 6.7, 10.9 and 17.3 seconds, which do not divide into one another, so the idle drift never visibly repeats.

The elastic cord is most of what separates a string from a rod. The charm hangs from the cord rather than being welded to it, which is why a flick makes it whip. The charm on this page runs the same numbers as the one on the desktop.
